# Developer Relations & Content Lead — Lyzr

**Location:** Bangalore (hybrid)
**Reports to:** Programme Manager
**Works closely with:** Instructors, video editors, programme ops

---

## About Lyzr

Lyzr is building the agentic AI platform for enterprise — an SDK and a SaaS surface for shipping production AI agents. We run structured programmes that take builders from "I know what an agent is" to "I've shipped one in production." These span education, cohort programmes, and community — across developer and business audiences. We're scaling fast and need someone who can keep community, content, and learners moving in sync.

## The role in one sentence

You are the connective tissue between our content, our community, and our participants — writing scripts and exercises, running Discord, meeting people where they're stuck, and closing the loop back into what we ship next.

## Why this role exists

Most programmes treat content creation, community management, and learner success as three separate jobs. At this stage, that's a recipe for silos and slow feedback loops. One person who can write a script, ship a hands-on exercise, answer a stuck participant in Discord, and push fixes back into the curriculum closes the loop tightly enough that the content actually gets better every week. That person is you.

This is a broad role — intentionally. It will probably split into multiple roles as we scale. You get first pick of which direction you want to grow.

## What you'll own

| Area | What it looks like day-to-day |
|---|---|
| **Scripts & curriculum** | Turn teaching outlines into production-ready scripts across developer and business content |
| **Hands-on content** | Write code exercises, labs, starter repos, and sample notebooks that participants actually run |
| **Community** | Run the Lyzr Discord. Answer questions, unblock stuck participants, moderate, host discussions |
| **Developer outreach** | Post on dev Twitter/LinkedIn, engage with the AI/agents community, pitch to dev newsletters, DM creators |
| **Participant success** | Respond to stuck participants, surface friction patterns, own the feedback loop from "got confused" → "got updated" |
| **Content feedback loops** | Instrument what participants struggle with, drop off from, or love — turn signal into improvements |

## What you bring

- 3–6+ years in some combination of developer relations, developer advocacy, technical writing, education, or software engineering with a teaching bent
- **You actually write code.** Python fluency is required. You've shipped something real — a library, an app, a tool, a notebook that other developers used
- **Agentic AI fluency.** You've built with Lyzr, LangChain, LangGraph, LlamaIndex, CrewAI, or similar — enough to have opinions about what's good and what isn't
- **You can write.** Clear, opinionated, not corporate. You've published technical content — blog posts, docs, tutorials, threads
- **High empathy for beginners.** You remember being stuck and you don't talk down to people who are
- **Externally visible by default.** You're comfortable being the public face of the community — posting, replying, hosting, showing up
- **Self-directed.** You can run a workstream without being told exactly what to do next

## Nice to have

- Has run a Discord, Slack, or forum community before at meaningful scale
- Active presence on dev Twitter/X, LinkedIn, or Hacker News
- Prior DevRel or Developer Experience role at an AI/developer-tools company
- Experience building courses, cohort programmes, or educational content that landed with developers
- Understanding of both sides of the Lyzr product — SDK and SaaS platform
- A GitHub with projects, not just commits

## This role is not the right fit if

- Your background is marketing or communications — you need to have built things with code, not just written about them
- You prefer a clearly scoped, single-discipline role — this requires moving across writing, coding, and community in the same week
- Public-facing work makes you uncomfortable — posting, replying, and showing up externally is core to this role
- You work best with detailed briefs and structured handoffs — most of what you'll do here you'll define yourself

## What success looks like at 90 days

- At least one programme track ships with scripts and exercises you wrote or co-owned
- A Lyzr community Discord exists, has hundreds of members, and feels alive
- Participants who hit walls get unstuck in hours, not days
- There is a visible feedback loop where participant friction → content updates
- You are recognizably part of the agentic AI dev community — posting, replying, showing up
